Both algorithms were run on identical input: events [7500, 8000) of ColliderML ttbar_pu200, with cells zero-suppressed at 0.0002 GeV. Target particles satisfy pT >= 0.5 GeV, |eta| <= 0.88, and at least 10 calorimeter cells counted after zero-suppression. Truth is the exclusive partition: each cell belongs to the particle depositing the most energy in it. Truth particles and predicted clusters are matched one-to-one by maximising shared calibrated energy with scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment; unmatched truth particles count as inefficiencies and unmatched clusters as fakes. Cell energies are calibrated per subsystem (ecb 37.5, ece 38.7, hcb 45.0, hce 46.9). Layer indices come from a frozen geometry of ecb 48, hcb 36 layers. MaskFormer results use checkpoint epoch=003-val_loss=2.06527.ckpt at mask threshold 0.05 and object threshold 0.5; it was trained on events [0, 6000], disjoint from those reported here. A matched pair must share at least 0.05 of the smaller of the two energy totals, so a cluster merely grazing a particle is not counted as reconstructing it. Splitting and merging use a 0.1 threshold applied to calibrated energy rather than to cell counts. Uncertainties on fractions come from resampling events rather than particles, since particles within an event are not independent. Two reference clusterings are QUOTED AS BOUNDS rather than drawn: a resolution ceiling, in which target particles sharing more than half of the smaller one's energy are merged and then clustered perfectly, which bounds what these cells can support; and a geometric ceiling, in which an idealised method given the true particle count and shower axes assigns each cell to the nearest axis in angle and depth at the best of a scanned range of depth weightings. Only the first is a bound -- the second is one specific assignment rule that methods here exceed elsewhere, and is reported as a characterisation of nearest-axis assignment rather than as a limit.
